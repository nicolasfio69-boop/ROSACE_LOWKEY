At Espoo's matchmaking events,
companies send their needs the night before,
and every CV now sounds the same, because AI wrote it.
Rosace turns each claim into a question
you can check during the conversation.
Before the event, Kaarna Health, a small Espoo start-up,
opens the recruiter space and drops in its job post.
Claude reads it and proposes what matters,
citing the sentence each requirement comes from.
Nothing applies until the recruiter accepts.
The skills come from one shared framework:
twelve components, the same for every company.
The three majors are drawn in gold.
The recruiter stays in charge.
For a junior, Kaarna cares most about
flagging a late ticket early, so they swap one major.
Claude then writes level examples for this exact job,
so Intermediate means something concrete.
Technical skills sit beside the rosace.
Each requirement already says how it will be checked,
and Kaarna will meet two people.
Now the talent. Fatima was a pharmacy assistant,
then did a coding bootcamp.
Her CV stays on her device:
it is never stored, and never sent to AI.
It only points to clues. For each skill,
she answers three plain questions,
and she confirms her own level.
Where she has no real example,
she simply leaves the skill unanswered.
She keeps her card for six months, with her consent,
and sees her own rosace, never a rank or a score.
The organiser sees the whole event:
three other companies and ten talents.
Ties are never hidden; they go to a person.
For Kaarna, Fatima is suggested,
with the reasons behind it.
Profiles from other jobs are not pushed forward.
Every meeting is a human click, with a short reason.
At the event, the recruiter walks in
with questions that check each claim, not with a ranking.
After the talk, they note what they actually saw.
Observed stays separate from declared:
Fatima's own words are never overwritten.
Synthetic data only. Nationality, language, degree
and career path never enter a calculation.
Recruitment is high-risk under the EU AI Act,
so Rosace only suggests, and always shows why.
Stronger signal, less noise,
for Espoo's next matchmaking event.
